The nucleosynthesis of heavy elements (A > 56) beyond iron represents one of the most fascinating and complex phenomena in nuclear astrophysics. The rapid neutron-capture process (r-process), which is responsible for the production of the majority of the heavy elements observed in the universe, occurs in astrophysical sites characterized by extremely high neutron fluxes, such as neutron star mergers and core-collapse supernovae. Investigating the nuclear structure of exotic, neutron-rich nuclei far from stability is essential for understanding the r-process pathway and provides critical input for constraining the equation of state (EoS) of neutron stars -- astrophysical objects of profound significance. The EoS governs the macroscopic properties of neutron stars, including their mass-radius relation. While astronomical observations impose constraints on neutron star models at the macroscopic scale, experimental nuclear physics offers indispensable information on the microscopic nuclear parameters on which these models rely.\\
A comprehensive description of the r-process requires detailed knowledge of nuclear structure, the underlying nucleon-nucleon interactions, and the reaction dynamics in regions far from stability. In particular, a complete understanding of the observed heavy-element abundances demands accurate modeling of fission and its dynamics, which terminate the r-process and feed back into it through fission recycling.\\
This thesis, entitled “Nuclear Structure Investigations of Light Nuclei with the R3B Experiment,” presents detailed studies of advanced experimental techniques for probing key nuclear properties with high precision. The investigations were carried out using the R3B (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) setup during the commissioning experiments S444 and S455, performed as part of the FAIR Phase-0 campaign at GSI.\\
In summary, this thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of nuclear scattering observables and reaction mechanisms within the R3B experimental framework. It comprises (i) high-precision measurements of the charge-changing and total interaction cross sections for $^{12}$C + $^{12}$C collisions in the beam energy range of 400–800 MeV/nucleon; (ii) validation of the quasi-free scattering benchmark reaction $^{12}$C(p,2p)$^{11}$B in inverse kinematics as a tool for probing single-particle nuclear structure; (iii) an exploratory study of fission via quasi-free scattering as a novel approach to fully characterize the fission process complementary to coulex fission; and (iv) the development of advanced cluster reconstruction techniques for the CALIFA calorimeter employing machine-learning methods.\\
Together, these studies contribute to the refinement of experimental methodologies for the investigation of exotic nuclei and provide stringent benchmarks for theoretical models describing nuclear matter and its role in astrophysical environments.
