\section*{Abstract}
The nucleosynthesis of heavy elements (A > 56) beyond iron represents one of the most fascinating and complex phenomena in nuclear astrophysics. The rapid neutron-capture process (r-process) is responsible for the majority of heavy element production in the universe and provides a critical interface between astrophysics and nuclear physics, a unique "laboratory" to study nuclear reactions and properties far from stability. Moreover, neutron star mergers (NSMs), are recognized as one major astrophysical sites for the r-process. Neutron stars (NS), with their extreme densities and exotic composition, are of exceptional interest in this context. These astrophysical objects serve as a natural laboratory to explore nuclear matter under the most extreme conditions, described by the equation of state (EoS), which governs the macroscopic properties of matter inside neutron stars. While astrophysical observations provide macroscopic constraints on NS models, nuclear experiments at accelerator laboratories play a vital role in constraining the microscopic nuclear physics parameters embedded within these models. A comprehensive understanding of the r-process requires detailed knowledge of nuclear structure, the underlying interactions and reaction dynamics in regimes far from nuclear stability.\newline
%However, experimental constraints on critical observables, such as fission yields and fission barriers, are limited due to the challenges associated with producing and studying these neutron-rich nuclei in laboratory settings.
This thesis, titled "Nuclear Structure Investigations of Light Nuclei with the R3B Experiment", addresses detailed investigations on the state of the art experimental techniques to study key properties of the most exotic nuclei with highest precision. These investigations were conducted with the R$^3$B (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) setup during the commissioning experiment S444 as part of the FAIR Phase-0 campaign at GSI.\newline

In summary, this thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of nuclear scattering observables and reaction mechanisms within the R3B experimental framework. It comprises (i) the high precision measurement of the charge-changing and total interaction cross sections for $^{12}$C + $^{12}$C collisions in the beam energy range of 400-800 MeV/nucleon, (ii) the validation of the quasi-free scattering benchmark reaction $^{12}$C(p,2p)$^{11}$B in inverse kinematics as tool to probe the single particle structure of nuclei, and (iii) the development of advanced cluster reconstruction techniques employing machine learning for the CALIFA calorimeter.\newline 
Together, these studies contribute to the refinement of experimental methods for the investigation of exotic nuclei and provide stringent benchmarks for theoretical descriptions of nuclear matter.
