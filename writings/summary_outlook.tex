\section{Summary and Outlook}
In this work, three aspects of the R3B progam were investigated: experimental work, analysis tools and instrumental developments. These advancements are essential for the next generation of high-precision measurements to be carried out at the FAIR facility, which are needed to probe the structure of exotic nuclei, to refine the nuclear physics inputs for \emph{r}-process modeling, and, ultimately, to advance our understanding of the origin of the heavy elements in the Universe.\\

\textbf{Benchmark Measurements for the EoS Experiments at FAIR}\\
One of the central scientific objectives of the R3B experiment is to deliver stringent experimental constraints on the nuclear equation of state (EoS), in particular on the density dependence of the symmetry energy near saturation. A key quantity in this context is the slope parameter $L$, which governs the pressure of neutron-rich matter and thus has direct implications for the neutron-skin thickness of heavy nuclei and for the macroscopic properties of neutron stars. A promising strategy to constrain $L$ is the measurement of neutron-skin thicknesses of exotic, neutron-rich nuclei through neutron-removal cross sections, combined with state-of-the-art reaction models that relate these observables to the underlying symmetry-energy parameters.\\
These reaction models must be benchmarked against systems for which the nuclear density distributions and free nucleon-nucleon cross sections -- the key ingredients of the models -- are known with high accuracy. The total interaction cross section of the $^{12}$C+$^{12}$C system represents an ideal test case, since the charge radius and density distribution of $^{12}$C are well established from decades of scattering experiments. A second objective is to benchmark the experimental setup itself, quantify sources of systematic uncertainty, and establish robust analysis methods to overcome those.\\
While high-precision measurements of the total interaction cross section for $^{12}$C+$^{12}$C collisions have already been performed with the R3B setup in high-resolution mode -- characterized by long flight paths and operation under vacuum conditions --by Ponnath et al.~\cite{ponnath2024measurement}, the measurements presented in this thesis for beam energies of 400-800 AMeV provide an independent verification carried out in the maximal-acceptance configuration of the detector system.

The enhanced cross section values measured at 400~AMeV remain under investigation within the collaboration; potential detector-performance or efficiency issues in TWIN--MUSIC are currently being evaluated.\\
The precision achieved in this work -- better than 1\% -- demonstrates that the R3B setup is capable of performing measurements with uncertainties below the 2\% threshold required to constrain the symmetry-energy slope parameter $L$ at the $\pm$10 MeV level~\cite{aumann2017peeling}. Building on this result, future experiments with tin and lead isotopes over a wide range of neutron-to-proton ratios are planned to advance the program of constraining the symmetry energy through neutron-removal observables.\\
A further experiment proposal at R3B is currently under development with the objective to more precisely constrain the neutron pressure and the density dependence of the nuclear symmetry energy by measuring neutron-skin thicknesses and complementary reaction observables for Sn and Pb isotopes. The inclusion of \(^ {208}\mathrm{Pb}\) as one of the projectiles is particularly valuable, as it provides a direct link to the PREX results and may help to address the existing tension between the PREX and CREX measurements discussed in the introduction.\\
The proposed programme will be carried out at R3B at the new High-Energy Cave (HEC) and will combine measurements of total reaction, charge-changing, and neutron-removal cross sections with Coulomb-excitation data. These experimental observables will be interpreted alongside state-of-the-art reaction theory and \emph{ab initio} density predictions.\\
Owing to the kinematically complete detection capabilities and the multi-observable strategy, the proposed experiment aims to deliver stringent and largely model-independent constraints on the slope parameter \(L\) of the symmetry energy. In doing so, it is expected to provide new insights into the nuclear equation of state (EoS) at high isospin asymmetry and over an extended density range.

\textbf{QFS and Complementary Fission Studies}\\
The second part of this thesis focused on quasi-free scattering (QFS) as an experimental method to probe single-particle structure in nuclei. The feasibility study of the exclusive reaction ${}^{12}\mathrm{C}(p,2p){}^{11}\mathrm{B}$ demonstrated that a clean identification of this channel is achievable and that all reaction products can be measured kinematically completely within the R3B setup. Furthermore, a distinctly Doppler-corrected $\gamma$-ray spectrum was obtained for the transitions from excited states of the ${}^{11}\mathrm{B}$ fragment to its ground state. The subsequent S455 experiment extended this method to study fission via QFS in the reaction $^{238}$U(p,2p)fission. The analysis conducted in this work shows that QFS-induced fission provides a powerful tool to directly relate fission-fragment yields and distributions to the corresponding excitation energies of the fissioning nucleus. With the development of a high resolution Target Recoil Tracker (TRT) the excitation energy resolution will be improved to the sub-MeV range. Together with a detailed statistical analysis, this will enable the mapping of the potential energy surface (PES), particularly for proton-rich heavy nuclei, and in the future also for isotopes on the neutron-rich side of the nuclear chart. Complementary measurements of Coulomb-excitation-induced fission further revealed the existence of a new “island of asymmetric fission,” driven by shell effects in the light fragments~\cite{morfouace2025asymmetric}. Ongoing and future proposals aim to refine the boundaries of this island and, in the case of QFS-induced fission, to extend these studies to exotic nuclei far from stability.\\

\textbf{Instrumental Development}\\
The forthcoming move of the R3B setup to the new High-Energy Cave (HEC) at FAIR will significantly enhance the experimental capabilities. The larger experimental area will allow for an optimized detector geometry, and ongoing developments in detector technologies -- combined with advanced data-analysis methods such as machine learning, as demonstrated in this thesis for cluster reconstruction in the CALIFA calorimeter -- are expected to further enhance the achievable precision and efficiency. In particular, machine-learning approaches that exploit large training datasets and the possible integration of information from multiple detector systems, such as CALIFA and the TRT, offer additional potential for improving energy and spatial resolution as well as overall detection efficiency.\\
All these instrumental developments, together with the newly developed analysis tools, will contribute significantly to the promising experimental campaigns planned for the early-science program at FAIR, scheduled to begin in 2028.
